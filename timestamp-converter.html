Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about Unix timestamps

A Unix timestamp (also called epoch time) is the number of seconds that have elapsed since January 1, 1970 at 00:00:00 UTC. It is a widely used standard in computing for representing a moment in time as a single integer, making it easy to store, compare, and calculate time differences.

For example, the timestamp 1700000000 corresponds to November 14, 2023 at 22:13:20 UTC.

Enter your Unix timestamp (in seconds or milliseconds) into the "Timestamp to Date" field and click Convert. The tool automatically detects whether it is seconds or milliseconds and shows the result in UTC, local time, your chosen timezone, ISO 8601, and relative format.

Unix timestamps are traditionally in seconds (10-digit number, e.g. 1700000000). Modern APIs and JavaScript's Date.now() return milliseconds (13-digit number, e.g. 1700000000000).

This tool automatically detects the format: if the timestamp is 13+ digits, it treats it as milliseconds; otherwise as seconds.
